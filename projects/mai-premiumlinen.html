mai.premiumlinen

A social media strategy and content system for a one-person linen brand in Saigon: persona, funnel, 14-day plan, two automations, and AI-assisted video and carousel content.

Disclaimer: mai.premiumlinen is an existing brand that I don’t own. This is an independent course project: I chose the brand myself, and it wasn’t commissioned or paid for. I built the strategy, content and automations based on the brand’s public identity and what one person can realistically run. Nothing was posted on the brand’s accounts; I tested the automations on a separate account. Product photos come from the brand’s public posts and appear with the owner’s permission. I’m grateful she took the time to review it and share her feedback below.

Buyer persona

Every content idea is written for one person: a Gen Z student in Saigon who buys less, but better.

“I don't want more clothes. I want fewer things I actually love, and I want to know they were made with care, not just made cheap and fast.”Minh Trang · 20 · University student · Saigon, Vietnam

Goals

  • A small, considered wardrobe
  • Outfits that work for class & coffee dates
  • Clothes from brands that are open about how they make them
  • Light pieces that pack easily for travel

Pain points

  • Linen creases too easily
  • Tired of fast fashion
  • Sustainable clothes don’t fit a student budget
  • Not sure how to care for linen

Buying behaviour

  • Researches before buying
  • Buys less, chooses better
  • Trusts real-life photos over studio shots
  • Pays more for pieces she'll wear often

Prompts and what I changed

One iteration per funnel phase: what the first result got wrong and how I fixed it.

See The café that looked European
Problem
Google Flow kept generating a European-style café for clip 4. The persona lives in Saigon, so the scene didn't fit her world.
Revision
Person sitting at an outdoor Vietnamese-style coffee shop, low plastic stool or rattan chair, small round table, camera focused on her from a side angle while two friends sit across facing away with only their backs / shoulders visible. Tropical Saigon street setting with motorbikes or narrow alley visible softly out of focus in the background, dappled shade from a tree or storefront awning, warm humid daylight, relaxed candid chatting mood, natural unposed body language, editorial minimal style, no text.
Result
After several rounds, the clip showed a recognisable Saigon sidewalk café: low stools, motorbikes, tropical light.
Think A carousel that read like an FAQ
Prompt
Create a 6-slide Instagram carousel explaining why linen wrinkles, aimed at a Gen Z student audience hesitant about the fabric. Keep the tone premium and minimal.
Problem
Correct, but too explanatory. It read like a fabric-care FAQ and the cover had no emotional hook.
Revision
Rewrite the final carousel slide so it leads naturally into a DM conversation, without sounding like a sales prompt.
Result
“Still unsure? Ask us anything” beat the first draft (“Have questions about linen care? Send us a message!”). Shorter, and more like an invitation than customer service.
Do A sale caption that was too pushy
Prompt
Write a sales caption for a 25% off linen sale targeting Gen Z. Tell them to comment to get a discount code.
Problem
It worked, but sounded too aggressive for a premium slow-fashion brand.
Revision
Refine the copy to maintain a calm, 'quiet luxury' tone. Emphasize limited small-batch production rather than cheap discounts to protect brand equity.
Result
Urgency now comes from small-batch production, not the discount. That fits the persona's values and still gives her a reason to act.
Care “You've earned VIP access!”
Prompt
Write an Instagram Stories template for resharing customer photos, and a broadcast channel message offering early access to past buyers.
Problem
The broadcast opened with “You've earned VIP access!”, which sounds like a loyalty program and doesn't fit the brand's quiet tone.
Revision
Remove any language that sounds like a loyalty program (VIP, earned, points). Reframe the broadcast message as a personal, quiet heads-up rather than a reward. Keep it short.
Result
Early access now reads as a personal note. It also keeps the Care phase apart from the Do phase, which already carries the discount.
All video clip prompts (Google Flow)

Every clip used the same real product photo as a reference.

Clip 1: Slow close-up pan across folded linen fabric, soft natural morning light, hand gently touching texture, calm and minimal Clip 2: Person putting on a linen shirt in a bright bedroom, natural unforced movement, soft diffused light, no music sync needed Clip 3: Person walking outdoors in tropical humid setting, linen fabric moving naturally in light breeze, warm daylight, handheld camera feel Clip 4: (see the café iteration above) Clip 5: Extreme close-up of linen fabric crease, soft light, slow subtle movement, ending on a still frame

Text overlays (Claude):

Refine the text overlays to follow the AIDA formula. Each scene's overlay must be strictly under 7 words, readable in under 2 seconds, and emphasize 'quiet luxury' for Saigon's climate without using hype words.
Carousel image prompts (Gemini)

Every generated image used the real product photo from slide 1 as its reference. Slides 2 and 3 needed a second prompt: slide 2 copied slide 1’s framing, and the fabric comparison on slide 3 looked artificial.

Slide 2: Using this reference image for fabric texture, lighting, and color: close-up of the pleated fold detail on the linen trouser, shot from a slightly higher angle than the reference, showing the stitch line and fabric drape clearly, same soft natural window light, neutral warm-white tone, no text, editorial product photography style Slide 3: … side-by-side split composition; left half shows this linen fabric's natural woven texture in soft light, right half shows a smooth synthetic-looking fabric texture in the same lighting for contrast. Keep both halves minimal, neutral background, no text. Slide 4: … a woman wearing these white linen trousers, captured from the waist down or with her face out of frame, walking mid-stride so the fabric moves naturally and catches soft natural light. Same warm neutral tone as reference, minimal editorial style, no visible face, focus on how the linen fabric moves and creases naturally with motion

Automation

Two workflows that save a one-person brand time.

Comment-to-DM funnel

When someone comments “SUMMER” on the flash sale post, Manychat replies under the comment and sends a two-step DM: a greeting, then the sale link and size guide.

  1. TriggerA comment on the flash sale post.
  2. KeywordThe comment contains “SUMMER” or “summer”.
  3. Public replyManychat answers under the comment.
  4. Opening DMA greeting with a ‘Send me the link’ button.
  5. Follow-up DMCall to action with the sale link.

Tool switch. I planned this in Make.com, but Instagram limits comment triggers for third-party tools, so Make.com couldn’t watch comments on this account. I switched to Manychat, an official Meta partner built for comment-to-DM flows: the same call a solo owner would make when a tool can’t do the job.

What I learned

  • Plans change once you build. Make.com couldn’t trigger on Instagram comments, so I switched to Manychat (see the tool switch). Google Flow kept putting the café in Europe; it took several prompt rounds to get a Saigon street.
  • Every prompt has to count. Free accounts limit the runs, so I planned each prompt and named what was wrong before changing it.
  • AI can’t touch the product. It worked well for ideas, captions and automation. Linen’s texture had to come from real product photos, so every generated image used one as its reference.
  • Labelling is part of the job. All AI content is labelled under EU AI Act Art. 50.
  • A system beats more content. For a one-person brand, clear pillars, a few automations and repeatable workflows keep quality up without constant manual work.
  • What I’d hand off first. If the brand grew, I’d delegate carousel design and short videos, which are repetitive and slow. I’d keep strategy, positioning and brand voice, which hold everything together.
