Arre Mexican Essen: Team Cooking Event

A lead-generation landing page for a Mexican cook in Berlin. Company teams request a quote for a cooking course, in one short form.

Role
Solo
Context
DCI course, independent case study
Time
~3 days
Tools
HubSpot, Unsplash, Claude

About this project: Arre Mexican Essen is a real business in Berlin-Charlottenburg. I built this page as an outsider, for a course: I had no contact with the owner, and the page was not launched for the business. The reviews are quoted from Arre’s public website. The price and the response time are my assumptions.

Goal

Arre wants to sell its cooking course to companies as a team event, for example through a Google ad for “Teamevent Kochkurs Berlin” or a social post. A click on that ad shouldn’t land on the full website, with its menu, catering offers and blog. It should land on one page with one offer and one action: request a quote.

Goal

Company teams request a quote for a Mexican cooking course as their team event.

Type

Lead generation: one page, one offer, one action.

Target

5 quote requests per 100 visitors, a 5% conversion rate.

My SEO audit of Arre’s website showed what this page must avoid: the course page asks visitors to get in touch, but has no form, no phone number and no price. Why a quote, not a consultation call: every course needs a date, a group size and a venue before Arre can name a price, and the organiser wants that price first.

Audience

The course page leads with team building, so I wrote for the person who organises the event, not for the guests. The persona is my assumption, built from Arre’s offers and reviews.

Sandra, 34

Office manager at a Berlin company. Plans the team event for 12 colleagues, with a budget per person.

Wants

  • An activity where everyone takes part
  • Little planning effort
  • One invoice for the company

Worries

  • Dietary needs in the team
  • The price per person
  • Whether her date is free

She searches “Teamevent Kochkurs Berlin”. The ad would target that search, and the headline, page title and URL repeat it, so the page matches the ad she clicked.

Structure: 7 building blocks

Each block answers one question Sandra has before she sends the form. The page has no menu: the only way forward is the form.

  1. Headline“Das Teamevent, bei dem alle mitmachen”, with a subline: four hours, tacos, salsas, dessert, teams up to 16.Says in three seconds who the page is for and what they get.
  2. Hero imageColleagues cooking together at one counter, plus a taco photo.Shows the result, a team event, not just the food.
  3. Value propositionEveryone cooks · at your office or in the studio · one quote for the whole team.Benefits for the organiser, not a list of features.
  4. Social proofThree reviews from companies, quoted from Arre’s website.Trust from companies like Sandra’s.
  5. Call to action“Team-Angebot anfordern”, three times: under the subline, in the form, at the end.The label names what she gets, instead of “Submit”.
  6. FormSix short fields in German, beside the headline.Asks only what a quote needs.
  7. Reassurance“Kostenlos und unverbindlich” above the form button.Removes the last doubt right at the click.

The form: two steps

Step 1 asks only what a quote needs. Step 2 confirms the request and says what happens next. The form uses implicit consent for the request itself and a separate, optional opt-in for marketing.

  1. Name in one row. First and last name side by side keep the form short.
  2. Firma. Marks the request as a company booking and tells Arre who to invoice.
  3. Business email. The placeholder says why it’s needed: the quote goes there.
  4. Group size as a dropdown: bis 8 · 9–12 · 13–16 · mehr als 16. It sizes the quote and is quick to tap on a phone.
  5. Preferred date, optional. Many teams ask before they have a date.
  6. Course updates, optional and unticked. The request doesn’t depend on it.
  7. One line before the click answers “does it cost anything?” and “what happens to my data?”
  8. The button names the result, “Team-Angebot anfordern”, in the brand pink.
  9. The thank-you message confirms the request and sets the expectation: a quote by email within 24 hours.

Brand kit

The colours come from the Arre logo. The logo pink with white text has only 3.4:1 contrast, so the buttons use a darker pink with 6:1. Pink appears only on buttons, so the eye finds the action first.

What I changed after testing

I tested the published page at laptop and phone width, then fixed what slowed a visitor down. These changes mattered most.

  1. A button on the first screenThe form button sat two screens down on a laptop, and three to four on a phone, after both photos. I added a button under the subline and linked it to an anchor module placed right above the form (#form-banner). One tap now jumps straight to the form, on any device.
  1. Lighter photosBoth Unsplash photos came in far too large for a page that most visitors open on a phone. I resized them to 1440 × 960 px before uploading.

  1. Real reviews instead of a stock testimonialThe template came with a made-up customer (“Neil Kumar, VP of Marketing”). I replaced it with three company reviews from Arre’s website, and a note that they come from catering events.
  2. Pink only for actionsThe benefit titles were pink like the buttons. I made them dark, so the button is the first thing you see when you squint at the page.

The landing page

Limits of this case study

  • No contact with the owner. The price stays a placeholder: “ab [€__] pro Person”.
  • “Angebot in 24 Stunden” is my assumption, not Arre’s promise.
  • The reviews describe catering for companies, not the cooking course. The note under the cards says so.
  • The photos are Unsplash stock images, not Arre courses.
  • No ad ran, so the page got no real traffic and the 5% target is not measured.

In a real project, I would add a course photo, a review from a company course and the confirmed price, run a small ad campaign, then A/B test the headline once visitors arrive.
